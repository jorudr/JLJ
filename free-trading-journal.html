What a free trading journal should cover

A free trading journal is worth using if it covers the fundamentals properly: recording every trade with context, attaching screenshots and notes, and giving you a clear view of your results over time. It does not need every advanced feature to be useful — it needs the basics to be solid, since those are what build the habit of reviewing your trading in the first place.

What to expect at no cost

  • Trade logging. Record entries, exits, position size and outcome for every trade.
  • Screenshots and notes. Attach chart screenshots and written context so a trade makes sense when you look back at it weeks later.
  • Tags and decision context. Mark setups, mistakes and conditions so recurring patterns become visible without re-reading every entry.
  • Basic performance tracking. See win rate, average result and account growth without needing a spreadsheet.

Where the free tier reasonably stops

Advanced tooling — statistical pattern detection, visual strategy building, Monte Carlo robustness testing — takes real engineering to build and is usually reserved for a paid tier. In JORU, that's Karl-Nikolai I (which scans your trade history for the specific conditions actually costing you money and tracks whether you fix them), the Genesis Matrix (a visual graph for building and testing strategy rules), and Robustness Analytics (simulated equity paths to estimate risk of ruin and drawdown). That's a reasonable line: free should get you journaling consistently, which is the part that matters most early on, before statistical analysis has enough trade history to be meaningful anyway.

Getting the habit right first

The biggest difference in outcomes is rarely the software tier — it is whether a trader actually logs every trade, including the losing and the embarrassing ones. A free trading journal that gets used beats a paid one that gets abandoned after two weeks. Once you have a few months of consistently logged trades, that's when pattern-detection and strategy analytics tools have enough data to actually tell you something.
