## What trading journal software actually needs to do

Most trading journal software stops at logging entries, exits and P&L. That covers record-keeping, but it does not answer the question that matters: which parts of your process are working, and which ones are quietly costing you money.

Useful trading journal software should let you connect three layers — the trade itself, the strategy rules behind it, and the performance data that comes out of it — so you can trace a losing month back to a specific rule, setup or habit instead of guessing.

## Core features to look for

- **Structured trade logging.** Screenshots, tags, notes and decision context attached to every entry, not a spreadsheet row with no story behind it.
- **Strategy management.** The ability to define entry and exit rules as a strategy, save versions of it, and compare how each version performs over time.
- **Deep trade analytics.** Execution quality, risk used per trade, adverse excursion and missed opportunity — detail that goes beyond win rate and profit factor.
- **Equity and drawdown tracking.** A clear view of account growth that separates genuine progress from short-term variance.
- **Broker and exchange integration.** Import from MetaTrader 5, Bybit, Binance, Kraken or Interactive Brokers instead of re-entering trades by hand.

## Spreadsheets vs. dedicated software

A spreadsheet can log trades. What it cannot do is connect a trade back to the strategy version that produced it, flag recurring execution errors automatically, or simulate how a rule change would have affected past results. That gap is exactly what dedicated trading journal software is built to close.
